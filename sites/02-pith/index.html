We press the whole blood orange, skin included, so you get the bitter edge that juice leaves behind. Then water, bubbles and a pinch of sea salt. That's the can.

Build a box of 12

Three things go in. You can pronounce all of them.

  • One whole fruit

    Pressed with the peel on. The pith is where the bitterness lives, and bitterness is what makes it taste like a drink for grown-ups.

  • Sparkling water

    From a spring in the Mendips, carbonated hard. 4.2 volumes of CO₂, which is closer to champagne than to cola.

  • A pinch of sea salt

    0.2 grams of Cornish salt per can. You won't taste salt. You'll taste more fruit.

From tree to can in nine days

  1. Day 1

    Picked ripe

    Our growers in Sicily, Kōchi and the Florida Keys pick to order. Nothing sits in cold storage.

  2. Day 4

    Pressed whole

    The fruit is washed and pressed in one piece. Peel oils and pith go in with the juice.

  3. Day 5

    Fizzed cold

    We carbonate at 2°C because cold water holds more bubbles, and more bubbles carry more smell.

  4. Day 9

    At your door

    Canned in Somerset and posted in a box that fits through a letterbox slot sideways. Mostly.
